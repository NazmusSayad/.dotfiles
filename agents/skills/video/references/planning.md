# Planning

## Audience

Decide who is watching before anything else. Every later choice (story, features, claims, pace, captions, audio) serves them.

- Name the audience concretely by what they do and use, not "users".
- Write down what they already know, what they care about, and what problem they feel today.
- Decide what they should think or do after watching, and build toward it.
- Decide where they will watch. That sets the length and whether the video must work without sound.
- Speak their language: show the tools and workflows they use, and use their words in captions. Skip anything they don't care about, even if it's impressive.
- If the audience isn't clear from the request or the product, ask the user.

## Story

- Say what the product is in one line, show why it matters, then show how to get it.
- Pick a handful of features that do something visible on screen. A visible result beats text output.
- Find the boldest true claim and give it an attention hit: a statement, then a short beat that underlines it.
- Let length follow content. Don't squeeze a scene to hit a target length.

## Claims

- Check every claim against the code or a real run before it goes in the storyboard.
- Footage from one environment can stand in for another only if the product behaves the same there. Never present footage as something it isn't.

## Scenes

- One idea per scene, kept simple: show the action and its first visible result, then move on. Don't wait for long processes to finish.
- Plan what real material each scene needs before recording.

## Storyboard

Write a storyboard file in a folder outside the product repo: the audience, the action viewers should take, where they'll watch, the rules, and a scene table (rough time, what is shown, the real source, the raw file). When several agents work in parallel, it is their shared brief.

## Takes

For each scene, plan one raw take: what happens, in what order, and what the screen looks like before it starts. Log roughly when each moment happens so the edit knows where to look; exact cut points come from frames.
