# Design

## Palette

- Take colors from the product itself, from its real theme or brand files rather than by eye. If it has none and the user hasn't said anything, choose a restrained palette that looks good and fits the product.
- Use a near-black background, white and grey text, and one flat accent color for the words and symbols that matter most.
- No multicolor gradients, colored glows or rainbow borders. They read as AI-made.

## Background

Give the plain background depth without adding color: a few layers that are each barely visible, such as faint drifting light, a subtle pattern that fades toward the edges, a vignette and light grain. Together they stop it from looking flat.

## Type

- One clean sans family for headlines and captions, and the product's own font for anything taken from the product. Load fonts from local files.
- Headlines are heavy with tight letter spacing. The biggest claims go very large.
- Captions over footage get a soft dark shadow so they stay readable on any frame.
- Keep captions short, one idea each, and on screen long enough to read twice.

## Layout

- Present footage in a simple, generic window or device frame with rounded corners and a soft shadow. The frame is plain chrome, never an imitation of the product's UI.
- Vary the layout from shot to shot, for example the title above the footage, or beside it with the footage tilted slightly toward it.
- Text-only cards use the same type, palette and background as everything else.
